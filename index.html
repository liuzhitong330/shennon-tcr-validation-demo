Can a TCR batch prove both activity and specificity?

A small planning notebook that connects transduction quality, donor-balanced co-culture design, and orthogonal T-cell readouts.

Built by Cathy Liu for the Research Associate / Senior Research Associate position

Batch check Dose series Assay design Evidence

ShennonBio’s published MAGE-A1 program moves from single-cell discovery to a demanding validation chain: engineered Jurkat cells, transduced primary T cells from multiple donors, activation-marker flow cytometry, cytokine secretion, target-cell killing, and finally in vivo tumor control. A useful research-associate tool should keep the biological controls and the cell arithmetic visible at every step.

I built this notebook to answer one practical question: is an engineered T-cell batch ready for a functional assay, and does the proposed experiment distinguish potency from nonspecific activation?

The practical inference

A high transduction percentage is only a starting condition. A convincing lead should remain reproducible across donors, turn on several independent functional readouts, and stay quiet when either antigen or restricting HLA is absent.

Why this fits the position

The job asks for primary T-cell isolation and engineering, lentiviral transduction, mammalian cell culture, flow cytometry, ELISA, and molecular cloning. This notebook makes the handoff between those tasks explicit: construct and batch quality determine whether a carefully controlled functional assay can be interpreted at all.
